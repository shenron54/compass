title: UCITS ETFs and tax for a Hong Kong investor

This is general background, not tax advice. Check your own situation.

**Hong Kong's side is simple.** For individuals there is no capital gains tax and no tax on
dividends received. What you lose to tax mostly happens at the source: in the US, and inside the
fund.

**US dividend withholding.** US companies withhold tax on dividends paid to foreign investors.
There is no US–Hong Kong income tax treaty, so a Hong Kong resident holding US stocks or US-listed
ETFs directly typically suffers **30%** withholding. That applies, for example, to the dividend
on Alphabet shares held directly.

**Irish-domiciled UCITS ETFs** (for example EQQQ, VUAA, CSPX or SWRD) hold the US shares through an Irish
fund. Under the US–Ireland treaty the fund suffers **15%** on US dividends, and Ireland does not
withhold again when paying non-Irish investors. On a 1.5% dividend yield, the gap is about 0.2%
a year, compounding.

**US estate tax** is the bigger and lesser-known risk. Non-US persons owning *US-situs* assets
(US-listed stocks and ETFs) above **US$60,000** can face US estate tax on death, at rates up to
40%. Irish-domiciled funds are generally not US-situs assets. Direct US stock holdings are.

**Accumulating vs distributing.** Accumulating share classes (VUAA, SWRD) reinvest dividends inside
the fund. Distributing classes (EQQQ) pay them out to you. With no Hong Kong dividend tax, the main
difference is convenience and reinvestment discipline.

**Currency.** For an HKD-based account holding USD- or GBP-priced assets: The HKD
is pegged to the USD between 7.75 and 7.85, so USD assets carry little currency risk for you.
GBP-quoted funds such as EQQQ are still US companies underneath: the London price simply moves
with GBP/USD.
