Owning a Nasdaq-100 fund *and* individual tech stocks means owning those companies more than once.
**Look-through exposure** adds up what you hold directly and what you hold inside each fund.

**Example:** if 40% of a portfolio is in a Nasdaq-100 fund and Alphabet is about 7.5% of that
index, the fund holds 3% of the portfolio in Alphabet. Add a 10% direct Alphabet position and the real
exposure is 13%, not 10%. A broad S&P 500 fund at 10% (Alphabet about 4.5% of it) adds
another 0.45%.

**Sector concentration** works the same way. About half of the Nasdaq-100 is Information Technology
and another sixth is Communication Services (where Alphabet and Meta sit). A portfolio that looks
"diversified across five holdings" can be about 70% exposed to one theme.

**Why it matters:**
- **Correlation in crises.** In 2022, tech stocks fell together. Owning several of them gave no
  protection.
- **Single-company risk.** One company's bad quarter, a regulatory ruling or a lost product cycle
  can move 15% of your portfolio.
- **Behaviour.** Concentration you haven't measured feels like conviction until a drawdown.

**What compass shows:** the portfolio page lists look-through sectors and companies, using the
weights in `compass/defaults/lookthrough.yaml`. Those are approximate index weights with an as-of
date, so refresh them from the fund factsheets every few months. The tech-cap rule turns your
chosen limit into an alert.

Concentration isn't wrong. Most great fortunes are concentrated. But it should be a choice made
deliberately, with a written limit, rather than something that drifts in as the winners grow.
