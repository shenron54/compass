title: The labour market and the Sahm rule
order: 4
summary: Rising unemployment is the clearest sign a recession has started.

Recessions show up in jobs. Companies cut hiring, then cut staff, and laid-off workers spend
less, which feeds the cycle.

**The Sahm rule** (from economist Claudia Sahm) triggers when the three-month average of the US
unemployment rate rises 0.5 percentage points or more above its low of the previous 12 months.
Before 2024 it fired early in every US recession since 1970 with almost no false alarms. Note
what that means: it says a recession has *started*, not that one is coming.

**Its first famous miss:** the rule triggered in mid-2024, but no recession followed. Sahm herself
pointed out that unemployment was rising partly because of a large increase in labour supply
(immigration), not because of layoffs. Every rule is fitted to past conditions and can break when
the economy changes.

**Initial jobless claims** are weekly filings for unemployment benefit, the most timely labour
data there is. compass uses the four-week average to smooth out holiday noise and compares it with
its 52-week low. A rise of 15–30% from the low has often come before a rise in the unemployment
rate itself.

**Using it with stocks:** by the time the Sahm rule fires, markets have often already fallen a
lot, and the bottom can come within months. In 2008 the rule fired in early 2008, and the bottom
came in March 2009. That's a reason to have your drawdown ladder and cash reserve decided
beforehand, so that bad jobs headlines don't freeze you at the moment the rules say to deploy
cash.
