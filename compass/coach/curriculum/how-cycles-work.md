An economic cycle has four rough phases:

1. **Expansion.** Jobs and profits grow, and credit is easy to get.
2. **Late cycle.** Growth is still positive, but the central bank has tightened, the yield curve
   flattens or inverts, and valuations are often high.
3. **Recession.** Unemployment rises, profits fall, and credit spreads blow out.
4. **Recovery.** Rates are cut, spreads narrow, and stocks rise long before the jobs figures improve.

**Markets move before the economy.** Stocks are a bet on future profits, so they usually bottom
*during* a recession, months before unemployment peaks. In 2009 the S&P 500 bottomed in March and
US unemployment peaked in October. In 2020 stocks bottomed on 23 March, while the economy was
still shutting down. Waiting for the economic data to look good has usually meant buying well
off the lows.

**No single indicator times the cycle.** Each one has false alarms and long lead times. compass
groups them on three axes, which often disagree:

- **Conditions:** is the economy or the financial system under strain? (yield curve, credit
  spreads, financial conditions, VIX, the Sahm rule, jobless claims, inflation)
- **Valuation:** how much are you paying for each dollar of earnings? (Shiller CAPE, distance
  above trend)
- **Trend:** is the market itself rising or falling? (the 200-day average, drawdown from the high)

Some typical combinations:
- Calm conditions, stretched valuation and an uptrend: often a long late-cycle run that can go
  on for years.
- Stressed conditions, cheap valuation and a downtrend: often close to the best long-run entry
  points, and also the scariest time to buy.

Your rules turn these readings into actions written down in advance, so the decision is made
when you are calm rather than in the middle of a sell-off. The history view on each rule shows
how it would have behaved in past cycles. Read it before trusting a threshold.
