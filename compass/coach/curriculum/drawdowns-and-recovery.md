title: Drawdowns and how long recoveries take

A **drawdown** is how far a price is below its previous high. The usual vocabulary is:
- −5%: a pullback, which happens most years.
- −10%: a correction, which happens about once every one or two years.
- −20%: a bear market.

**The Nasdaq-100's history** is a reminder of what a tech tilt means:
- **2000–02:** about −83%. It took roughly 15 years to regain the March 2000 high in price terms.
- **2007–08:** about −54%.
- **2020:** about −30% in a few weeks, recovered within months.
- **2021–22:** about −35% as rates rose, recovered in 2023–24.

The S&P 500 fell about 57% in 2007–09 and 34% in 2020.

**The maths of recovery is asymmetric.** A 20% fall needs a 25% gain to recover, a 50% fall needs
100%, and an 83% fall needs about 490%. That's why the size of the deepest drawdown you can sit
through matters more than the average return.

**Valuation decides recovery time.** Recoveries were fast when the market fell from a fair
valuation (2020) and painfully slow when it fell from an extreme one (2000). Read the drawdown
together with CAPE.

**Using drawdowns to buy.** A drawdown ladder commits cash in steps (for example 25% at −10%, 35% at
−20% and 40% at −30%), so you buy more the cheaper the market gets without guessing the bottom.
The rule's history view shows every past cycle, the tranches it reached, and the 1, 3 and 5-year
returns afterwards. Pay attention to the cases where −30% became −50%: the ladder still works
then, but only if the last tranche isn't spent too early.
