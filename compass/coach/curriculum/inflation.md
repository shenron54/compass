title: Inflation and money supply
order: 5
summary: Why inflation decides how much room the Fed has to rescue markets.

---
**CPI inflation** is the year-on-year change in consumer prices. The Fed targets about 2% (on a
related measure, PCE). Inflation matters to investors mainly through what it lets the Fed do:

- With inflation **near target**, the Fed can cut rates quickly when growth weakens. That "Fed
  put" helped the recoveries in 2019 and 2020.
- With inflation **well above target** (above 4%), the Fed may have to keep rates high, or raise
  them, even while markets fall. That happened in 2022, when stocks and bonds fell together, and
  is the 60/40 investor's worst case.

**Inflation and valuations.** High and unstable inflation has historically come with low
price-to-earnings ratios: the 1970s ended with CAPE near 7. Low, stable inflation supports high
multiples. A return of inflation is one of the main risks to an expensive, tech-heavy market.

**M2 money supply** is cash and near-cash in the economy. It grew about 25% in 2020–21 (pandemic
stimulus) and then actually shrank in 2022–23, which is rare. Its link to inflation and stocks is
loose and much debated, so compass shows it for context only, never as a signal.

**For an HKD investor:** the peg ties Hong Kong to US monetary policy, so US inflation and rate
decisions shape your local mortgage and deposit rates as well as your US holdings.
