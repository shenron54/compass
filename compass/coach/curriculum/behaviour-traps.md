---
title: The behavioural traps
order: 14
summary: Most long-term underperformance is behaviour, not stock picking.
indicators: [vix, ndx_drawdown]
---
Studies of fund flows (for example DALBAR's annual reports and Morningstar's "Mind the Gap") keep
finding the same thing: the average investor earns less than the funds they own, because they buy
after rises and sell after falls. The gap is often 1–2% a year.

**Traps to watch for in yourself:**
- **Recency.** Assuming the last two years will continue. In 2021 that meant tech forever; in late
  2022 it meant tech is finished.
- **Loss aversion.** Losses hurt about twice as much as gains please, so you sell to stop the pain
  near the bottom.
- **Action bias.** Feeling that you must *do* something when markets move. Most days the right
  action is none.
- **Anchoring.** Refusing to sell (or buy) until a price returns to the one you paid.
- **Confirmation.** Reading only what supports the position you already hold.
- **Overconfidence after wins.** A good year feels like skill. It is usually the market.

**Defences compass is built around:**
- **Rules written in advance.** A threshold chosen on a calm day is better than a decision made
  while the VIX is above 40.
- **The history view.** Seeing how a rule behaved across 20–40 years of real cycles, including its
  failures, builds the patience to follow it.
- **The decision journal.** Write down the thesis, the trigger and what you expect *before* acting.
  The 30, 90 and 365-day reviews judge the reasoning separately from the outcome. A good decision
  can lose money, and a bad one can get lucky.
- **Dashboard-only alerts.** Nothing pings you in the middle of a sell-off. You look when you
  choose to.

A simple test before any trade: *would I make this same decision if the market had not moved this
week?*
