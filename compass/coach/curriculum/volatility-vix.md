The **VIX** measures how much movement the S&P 500 options market expects over the next 30 days,
expressed as an annualised percentage. A VIX of 20 means daily moves of roughly 1.25% are
normal.

**Rough levels:**
- Below 15: calm, sometimes complacent.
- 15–20: normal. The long-run average is about 19–20.
- 20–30: elevated.
- Above 30: fear. The VIX closed above 80 in November 2008 and in March 2020.

**Why it helps a long-term buyer:** spikes in the VIX tend to happen near short-term lows, because
that is when forced selling peaks. Buying when the VIX is above 40 has historically produced good
1–3 year returns. It has also meant buying while prices were still falling: in 2008 the VIX first
spiked in October, but the market bottomed in March 2009.

**What it isn't:** the VIX describes the *present* mood. It doesn't forecast direction. Low VIX
periods can last for years (2017, 2023–24) while the market grinds higher.

**Your tech tilt:** the Nasdaq-100 usually moves about 1.2–1.4 times as much as the S&P 500, so
when the VIX doubles, your EQQQ and single stocks swing more than the headline index. Size your
cash reserve and drawdown ladder with those bigger swings in mind.
